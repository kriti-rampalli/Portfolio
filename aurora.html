Aurora

A sunrise-simulating wall art alarm clock with weather and ambient lighting.

A wall-mounted lifestyle concept combining sunrise lighting, ambient scenes, weather information and sound, supported by a tested mobile control interface. Four of us built it in a semester for people who want to start the morning with light instead of a phone.

The brief and the ambition

Wellness meets décor

Who it's for. People who invest in both wellness and aesthetics and want a sunrise lamp without another purely functional object in the room. Our beachhead was high-income millennials without kids who are intentional about their daily routines.

The initial concept. Intentionally ambitious:

Simulated sunriseSoundWeather informationAmbient lightingConnected controlsCustomizable wall décor

The constraint. Roughly $25–30 in added team funds beyond the free lab inventory. We committed to the vision before we knew what that would support.

We committed before we knew which parts of that ambition we could realistically prove.

Interface design

Designing the app: familiar on purpose

For an already unfamiliar product, I chose not to invent a new interaction language. I sketched a flow and wireframe on a whiteboard, then built a functional interactive prototype around familiar conventions from alarm and smart-light apps.

Navigation separates Home, Alarms, Ambient, Weather and Settings. Familiar alarm controls were extended with Aurora-specific choices: dawn duration, brightness, color temperature and wake-up sound.

Hardware and research

Hardware and research changed the product

Challenged

Light color was the wrong emphasis

A researcher studying the photocircadian system challenged our focus on color and wavelength and pointed to perceived brightness and source angle.

Added

A second display and a diffusion layer

The LED matrix gave useful brightness control, but its resolution could not show weather text clearly. We added a smaller display and improvised a diffusion layer to soften the pixels.

Cut

The decorative-art concept

As implementation pressure grew, the customizable wall décor was de-scoped. The final prototype was still bulkier than intended.

User testing

What 17 testers told us

Seventeen participants completed four timed tasks in sessions of about 15 minutes. Most tasks went well, but the custom ambient scene was the hardest, and one weather pattern did not read.

Telling the story

The pitch

A 3–5 minute pitch built on one idea: the same person, the same morning, told twice. Take one is a phone alarm, a rushed montage and a cold walk to work. Take two is a slow sunrise from Aurora, a calm montage, a glance at the weather module and a jacket.

Same person. Same morning. Different story.

Positioning

Sunrise lamps sit on your nightstand doing nothing. Smart displays demand your attention. Decorative lights just look nice. Aurora is all of these, without their compromises.

Honest note. The pitch shows the full vision, including an art mode the prototype never built. I also designed and built the product landing page.

What I learned

Ideation is not commitment. I would still brainstorm just as broadly, but add a feasibility checkpoint before defining the product: materials, technical capability, budget, time and a minimum demonstrable feature set.

Familiar beats novel when the product is already new to the user.
